Podcastify
any text, staged for two voices

A Python command-line tool, rebuilt in October 2026

A two-host episode of a whole book, with every claim tied to the sentences it came from.

Podcastify reads an EPUB or article end to end, pulls out key points that cite their source sentences, plans an episode of a set length, and has Claude write a conversation between a host and a co-host in which every factual line carries the ids of the sentences it rests on. A second model call then checks each line against those sentences; lines that fail are rewritten once or dropped. Free edge-tts voices render the result as an MP3 with a chapter per section.

How an episode is made

Map the whole source, pick an outline, write with citations

The old LLM mode sent the first 12,000 characters of a source to the Anthropic API and asked for a conversation. For Alice that is the first chapter and a half. The new pipeline reads everything, in four steps, each a separate model call:

1. Spans
The text is split into numbered sentences with character offsets. These are the only things a line can cite. Alice has 1,431 of them, grouped into 15 chunks of about 10,000 characters.
2. Map
Each chunk goes to the model with its sentences numbered, four chunks at a time. It returns key points, each citing the sentence ids it comes from and rated 1 to 3 for importance. Alice gave 169.
3. Reduce
The model sees every key point and picks about two per target minute, in source order, grouped into three to five titled sections. For six minutes of Alice it chose 17 points in four sections, from the rabbit-hole to the trial.
4. Script
The model writes the conversation from the outline and the cited sentences (plus one neighbor on each side). Each line comes back as JSON with a speaker, the text, whether it makes a claim about the source, and the ids it rests on.
5. Check
Every line, claim or not, is checked against its cited sentences; see the next section. Lines that fail are sent back once for a rewrite and checked again, and dropped if they still fail.
6. Audio
edge-tts renders each line, four at a time; ffmpeg joins them and writes the sections as ID3 chapter frames in the MP3, or as chapter atoms in an M4B with -o book.m4b.

All LLM calls go through the local claude -p CLI on a Claude subscription, with built-in tools off and no user settings loaded (--tools "" --setting-sources ""), so the model sees the prompt and nothing on disk. With ANTHROPIC_API_KEY set, the same prompts go to the Messages API instead. Rule mode, which needs no model at all, is still the default.

The grounding check

Two checks per line, and what they catch

The local check embeds the line and its cited sentences with sentence-transformers all-MiniLM-L6-v2 and flags a cosine similarity below 0.40 (a threshold set before any eval run), or any figure written in digits that does not appear in the cited text. It flagged 3 of 151 claim lines and agreed with the LLM verdict on 82% to 100% of claim lines per source, mostly because both call nearly everything fine. It is a cheap screen for a line that cites the wrong place, which the script writer rarely did.

The LLM check is a separate claude -p call that reads each line with its cited sentences (and the line before it, since a short answer leans on its question) and returns supported, partly, unsupported or no claim. Lines the writer marked as non-claims are checked too, against their two nearest outline sentences, so a factual line cannot skip the check by being labeled a reaction. Seven lines across the four episodes were claims the writer had not marked.

What failed was rarely an invented fact. The nine failures were a question that presupposed something from outside the cited sentences ("It sits right on the river"), a quote attributed to a source the cited sentence does not name, or an inference stated as fact ("the consequence is prison"). The check also made one plain mistake, visible in the episode above: in the last paragraph of Alice the sister pictures "this same little sister of hers" grown up, which is Alice. The checker read it as the sister picturing herself, marked a correct line "partly", and the line was dropped after its rewrite. The second checker, Opus, made the same misreading of the next line.

The eval

Four sources, three modes, five measures

Each source was run three ways at a six-minute target: rule mode reading from the start until it had used as many words as the LLM episode, the LLM pipeline with the grounding pass, and the same LLM draft before the grounding pass. Before any episode existed, Opus wrote 12 key points for each source from the full text; I checked the figures in them against the sources and committed them (eval/keypoints/). Opus then marked each point as covered (1), partly covered (0.5) or missing in each script.

"Not fully supported" is the share of claim lines that Opus, as a second checker kept out of the pipeline, rated partly or unsupported against their cited sentences. Length and words per minute come from rendering every script with edge-tts and measuring it with ffprobe.

Checking the checker

Twenty lines read by hand

eval/hand_check.py drew 20 claim lines at random (seed 19) from the four drafts, before any repair, and printed each with the sentences it cites and no verdict. I read all 20 and recorded my own verdict in eval/hand_check.json before scoring. My reading agreed with the pipeline's checker on pass or fail for 18 of 20 lines, and with Opus on 19. The two disagreements with the pipeline went both ways: it called a correct paraphrase of the end of Alice "partly", and passed "That points at the water, not the air", an inference the cited sentences about John Snow's pump do not state.

Twenty lines with one real failure among them says the checker passes good lines; it says little about how many bad lines it misses. That would need a sample built around failures.

Limitations

What four sources can and cannot show

Four sources, one run each, one six-minute target. The coverage gap between rule mode and LLM mode is large and consistent; the grounding numbers are small counts (8 lines of 172), and a different run would move them by a few lines. The key points, the coverage grading, the second check and the listenability judge are all Claude models, and the scripts are Claude's too, so the judge may favor writing like its own.

The grounding pass, as built, did not lower the second checker's not-supported rate. It removes what one model flags, which is a narrow filter: Sonnet and Opus mostly disagree about which lines are partly supported, and both are sometimes wrong. What the pass does give is a record: every line in the transcript carries its source offsets and verdict, so a reader can check any line in a few seconds.

A "supported" verdict means the cited sentences back the line, not that the line is a fair summary of the work. The outline step decides what an episode leaves out, and nothing checks that. Six minutes of Alice skip the pool of tears, the Caucus-race and the croquet game. Episodes ran 5.6 to 7.9 minutes against the six-minute target. edge-tts is an unofficial Microsoft endpoint; 7.2.8 returned HTTP 403 on 2026-10-26 and 7.3.1 works.
